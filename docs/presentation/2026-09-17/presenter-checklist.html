1 · Before anyone joins

2 · Open with the problem

Give the audience the reason to care before explaining the machinery.
Useful opening line: “DWARF is the security-testing workbench for asking whether the real Cardano infrastructure remains safe, compatible, and recoverable when inputs, peers, state, and infrastructure stop behaving nicely.”

3 · While presenting the deck

Keep the story moving; use the slides as evidence, not a script.

4 · Learn walkthrough

Show that the product teaches an operator how the system and evidence model work.
Learn ↗Architecture ↗Threat coverage ↗

5 · Operate walkthrough

Follow the operator loop instead of opening pages in arbitrary order.
Do not expose: configuration secrets, tokens, wallet data, private report URLs, or shell history. The presentation can explain these integrations without opening their sensitive values.

6 · Live real-node demo

Use the fresh, isolated scenario proven through the same GUI button.
Selected scenario: runtime-substrate-honest-baseline-docker-mode-example-smoke
Expected wall time is roughly 45 seconds on cardano-box. It creates two real Cardano-node 10.7.1 containers, observes them for 10 seconds, evaluates three assertions, retains evidence, and tears the temporary topology down.
Scenario runner ↗Scenario definition ↗Passing reference run ↗

7 · If the live path misbehaves

Preserve the presentation; do not turn the meeting into an incident call.
Never imply a failed launch found a node vulnerability. Setup, readiness, harness, workload, assertion, and target failures must be separated before security classification.

8 · Return to proposals and decisions

Make the client choices explicit after they have seen the product work.
